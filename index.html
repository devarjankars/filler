Rheology

It is the study and characterization of how materials deform and react under mechanical stress

Rheologic properties frequently used to characterize how fillers behave in soft tissue include3:

Elasticity (G′):

The ability of a filler to regain its original shape after deformation

Cohesivity:

Determines an HA dermal filler’s resistance to compression and stretching, influencing its projection and ability to retain shape under stress.

Manufacturing process details

HA is an essential component of the extracellular matrix of all adult animal tissues and naturally exists in tissues as a polymer4

  • The monomeric unit that composes HA polymer chains is identical regardless of origin (e.g., animal or bacterial)

The principal difference between HA sourced from animals (eg, chicken comb) vs HA sourced from bacteria (eg, streptococcus species) is the length of the polymer chain (n) and molecular weight4

  • Animal-based HA chains are generally longer than HA polymers sourced from bacteria (~4-6 MDa vs ~1.5-2.5 MDa, respectively)
  • Animal-sourced HA presents a small risk for hypersensitivity (eg, due to residual avian proteins)5

HA = hyaluronic acid; MDa = megadalton.
4. Tezel A and Fredrickson GH. J Cosmet Laser Ther. 2008;10:35-42. Supported by Allergan. 5. Zhai P, et al. Intl J Biol Macromol. 2020;151:1224-1239.

  • HA (also known as hyaluronan) is a polysaccharide (glycosaminoglycan) consisting of repeating D‑glucuronic acid and D-N-acetylglucosamine disaccharide units ‘HA monomer’), with a molecular weight of ~400 Da4
  • These disaccharide monomers link together into linear chains through beta-1,4 glycosidic bonds to form polymers4
    • The number of repeating disaccharides, n, in an HA polymer chain can reach 25,000 or more, creating a polymer with a total molecular weight of up to 10 MDa
  • Molecular weight of the starting HA polymer used in the manufacture of HA dermal fillers can impact the filler characteristics, e.g., water uptake and duration3

HA Concentration and Un-crosslinked HA: Effects on HA Filler Properties4

  • HA concentration and degree of crosslinking can affect elasticity, cohesivity, and water uptake
  • The viscosity and extrusion force of HA gels depend on a number of properties, including the degree of crosslinking, sizing method, average gel particle size and size distribution, amount of crosslinked and un‑crosslinked HA in the final product, and the manufacturing process itself
    • When all other factors are held constant, increasing the degree of crosslinking hardens the gel and increases viscosity and extrusion force.
    • Products manufactured using sieving to create gel particles of a defined size tend to have a higher viscosity and extrusion force than products developed with homogenization to produce gels of smooth consistency with a broad range of gel particle sizes
  • In order to facilitate injection of crosslinked gels, un-crosslinked HA can be added during the formulation to act as a lubricant and lower the elasticity during injection and reduce the required force to inject the product
    • While addition of un-crosslinked HA aids smooth injection of a dermal filler, it is rapidly metabolized after injection and therefore does not contribute to clinical persistence of the product

HA = hyaluronic acid.
4. Tezel A and Fredrickson GH. J Cosmet Laser Ther. 2008;10:35-42. Supported by Allergan.

  • HA chains exist in varying lengths and form random coils4
  • Large-molecular-weight HA coils entangle to form a viscous liquid4
    • Rapidly broken down in the body
  • Crosslinking transforms the viscous liquid into a viscoelastic gel4
    • More resistant to breakdown once within the body

Mechanical Properties of HA fillers: Viscosity and Extrusion Force2

Viscosity is the resistance to flow when shear stress is applied

  • Fillers are subjected to high levels of shear stress during injection; viscosity will affect extrusion force (i.e., ease-of-injection)
    • Extrusion force is a measure of the force needed to inject a filler at a fixed rate through a needle/cannula.
    • It is a function of gel viscosity and influenced by syringe, needle length, diameter, and injection rate.
  • The viscosity of dermal fillers decreases as greater forces are applied
    • Dermal fillers are designed with sufficiently low viscosity to allow injection with low-to-moderate extrusion force.
    • Once injected in the face, the forces are low enough that the elastic properties of the gel dominate.

HA = hyaluronic acid.

Elasticity (G′) Assay

G′ = gel elasticity (ability to regain original shape after deformation)

Factors that can influence G′1-3

  • Degree of HA crosslinking:

    More crosslinking will result in a stiffer gel (higher G′)

  • HA concentration:

    In HA fillers manufactured using the same technology and with the same degree of crosslinking, increasing HA concentration will result in a firmer gel (higher G′)

Unconstrained* Water Uptake Assay

Water uptake = ability of a gel to absorb water, increasing the overall volume

Factors that can influence water uptake1-3

  • Degree of HA crosslinking

    More crosslinking holds HA chains together more tightly, limits flexibility to move apart, and reduces water uptake capacity

  • HA concentration

    Assuming a consistent degree of crosslinking, HA molecular weight, post-crosslinking modifications, and other conditions, increased HA concentration will result in greater water uptake capacity
